### This is the end of the network as we know it, and we feel fine

**Links:** [Abstract](https://tnc26.geant.org/programme/#Tuesday/all/s1064) · [Slides](https://indico.geant.org/event/6/contributions/569/attachments/438/963/Context-Aware%20Network%20Orchestration%20in%20GE%CC%81ANT.pptx)

Aleksandr Kurbatov, Karel van Klink (GÉANT)

- **Why**: GÉANT has finished migrating its backbone packet layer to a new equipment vendor — and the interesting part is that the orchestration stack (the GÉANT Service Orchestrator, grown out of the Workflow Orchestrator programme) is what made a vendor swap of a pan-European backbone tractable. For anyone building network automation, the hard-won modeling decisions here transfer directly.
- **What**: A migration retrospective plus the orchestration model it enabled. The core of the presentation (per the slides) is how GÉANT models commercial peers: each peer is a *single entity* (ASN, prefix limit) that can hold multiple connections to different placement port types — IX port, private peering port, or transit port — explicitly avoiding a data-duplication anti-pattern. The same operator workflow produces different router configurations (communities, policies, BGP groups, prefix-limit handling) depending only on which port type the connection targets.
- **How**: A layered model in the orchestrator where connection *Type* is derived automatically from the target placement port; Ansible extracts the type, pulls the correct variables, and translates the orchestrator abstraction (one Peering Connection grouping IPv4+IPv6) into two independent BGP neighbor statements on the router. Per-connection Session States (Provisioning → Active → Disabled) act as predefined configuration shortcuts — e.g. Provisioning establishes BGP without route exchange, Disabled keeps all config in place but shuts down the neighbors. "One update, one workflow" propagates a peer change (e.g. prefix limit) across all of that peer's connections while leaving co-located peers untouched.
- **Where applicable**: GÉANT-specific backbone and orchestrator, but the patterns — model per organization not per port, port-type-derived config, session-state shortcuts, safe scoped deletion in multi-peer environments — are broadly applicable to any operator automating multi-site peering across IX, private, and transit contexts.

### Building AI That Works: ESnet's Pragmatic Approach to AI-Driven Operational Excellence

**Links:** [Abstract](https://tnc26.geant.org/programme/#Tuesday/all/s1068) · [Slides](https://indico.geant.org/event/6/contributions/579/attachments/508/1095/ESnet%20TNC26_%20AIops-1.pptx)

Edward Balas, Jon-Paul Herron (ESnet)

- **Why**: One of the few AIOps talks with deployed results rather than demos. ESnet built ORBIT (Operations Response & Business Intelligence Toolkit) to address growing scale with fixed NOC staffing, and reports what actually worked — including the unglamorous lessons.
- **What**: Two production applications: Intelligent Resolution Guidance, which proactively surfaces relevant documentation for incoming alarms/tickets, and Adaptive Incident Summarization, which generates audience-tailored ticket summaries. A concrete example in the slides: an operator asks what to do about a "Span Loss Out of Range – High" alarm and gets guided resolution. Future roadmap items include Drishti (early-alpha unsupervised log analysis: identify interesting events, cluster, generalize into behavioral sequences, summarize) and off-the-shelf Elasticsearch anomaly detection — multivariate for data movement (limited dimensionality, weak explainability) and univariate for predicting resource exhaustion ("preserves our engineers' weekends").
- **How**: Unified data access fusing ticketing (ServiceNow), Slack chatter (context that never made it into tickets), documentation, live metrics, and logs, with LLM agents and semantic search over semi-structured data via MCP. A representative query — "customers in Chicago report unstable service, summarize what might be responsible" — has the agent walk the customer database, backbone topology, ticket history, metrics, and operations chats before synthesizing an answer. Humans stay in the loop throughout.
- **Where applicable**: ESnet-scale research WAN, but the architecture (chat + tickets + docs + telemetry into one agent environment, semantic search/MCP over ops data) is general. Key lessons are stated plainly: treat prompts as code (version, QA — 25% speedup from refinement alone), plan for stochastic outputs, your first use cases won't be your strongest, and develop organizational policies for AI data access before capability sprawl.

### Machine Learning for Anomaly Detection in RNP Network Operations

**Links:** [Abstract](https://tnc26.geant.org/programme/#Tuesday/all/s1068) · [Slides](https://indico.geant.org/event/6/contributions/580/attachments/433/1024/TNC26%20%281%29.pptx)

### Discovering a DWDM network with Tomography

**Links:** [Abstract](https://tnc26.geant.org/programme/#Wednesday/all/s1276)

Annalisa Morea (Nokia), Jani Myyry (CSC/Funet), Kurosh Bozorgebrahimi (Sikt), Magnus Bergroth (SUNET)

- **Why**: Turns the coherent transponder from a transmission component into a sensing device that infers the physical network topology with no prior knowledge — directly relevant to anyone operating optical plant whose documentation drifts from reality. (from abstract only; the programme page was not retrievable)
- **What**: Nokia Bell Labs' "Tomography" method estimates fiber types, span lengths, and amplifier types from data the DSP already computes. In a February 2026 field trial on a multi-vendor Scandinavian network (CSC, Sikt, SUNET), a signal ran Tromsø→Espoo over two routes with characteristics unknown to the Nokia team, in C-band alongside live traffic, three wavelengths measured per route, using PSE-Vs (SFM6) transponders. Results: fiber length estimated with less than 4% error, good amplifier-type match, operators corrected erroneous database entries, and a path commissioning error was detected.
- **How**: The DSP analyzes received symbols to determine accumulated chromatic dispersion and nonlinearities; an algorithm exploits the non-commutative property between CD and nonlinear effects to convert this into power-versistance distance maps, and from those into span/topology estimates — no additional hardware, coexisting with revenue traffic.
- **Where applicable**: Requires coherent DSP transponders (Nokia PSE-Vs in the trial); the fiber plant itself was multi-vendor and heterogeneous. It's a partner (Nokia) session, but the field trial is independently hosted by three NRENs with named, verifiable outcomes — not a pitch.

### Churn: GÉANT's Next Iteration in Network Monitoring and Reporting

**Links:** [Abstract](https://tnc26.geant.org/programme/#Wednesday/all/s1073) · [Slides](https://indico.geant.org/event/6/contributions/594/attachments/442/956/TNC26_Churn.pdf)

Erik Reid (GÉANT)

- **Why**: A rare, well-argued reference architecture for telemetry pipelines. GÉANT redesigned its monitoring/reporting stack after hitting three predictable walls: duplicated collector configuration across SNMP/NETCONF/gRPC, no unified picture of what talks to the devices, and tech-stack coupling (every debate about Influx vs. ClickHouse vs. Elastic vs. Telegraf vs. gNMIc stalling progress).
- **What**: Churn is not another monitoring tool or datastore — it consolidates device access and ingestion (gNMI, SNMP, NETCONF, SSH, ICMP) behind small, repeatable message processors, and deliberately keeps storage/visualization choices at the processing level rather than the architecture level. A deliberate "middle path" between a premature framework and per-service pipelines: share only what converges, keep internals explicit (no "metadata" trap), accept some per-integration effort.
- **How**: Processors follow a consume → filter → transform → publish pattern over RabbitMQ exchanges, with strict internal consistency via Pydantic validation; internal Churn models never leave Churn. Horizontal scaling via round-robin task queues over shared exchanges. Notably, Prometheus is used as a lightweight *data-sharing* tool (via ROBIN to NRENs and the community) rather than as a monitoring platform — a trick worth stealing. EuroHPC reporting needs were absorbed by reusing the same pipelines.
- **Where applicable**: GÉANT-scale backbone, but the processor pattern and the design reasoning transfer to any observability pipeline rebuild. Source code and documentation are publicly available (GÉANT GitLab).

### Network Flow Monitoring: An Evaluation of Modern Collector Software

**Links:** [Abstract](https://tnc26.geant.org/programme/#Wednesday/all/s1073) · [Slides (PDF)](https://indico.geant.org/event/6/contributions/595/attachments/446/1135/TCN26-Flow-Collector-Evaluation.pdf) · [Slides (PPTX)](https://indico.geant.org/event/6/contributions/595/attachments/446/1136/TCN26-Flow-Collector-Evaluation.pptx)

Yannick Huber (BelWü / University of Stuttgart), with Benjamin Steinert, Lukas Pietzschmann, Gabriel Paradzik, Michael Menth (Tübingen, Ulm)

- **Why**: Firmly substantiated, quantitative tool evaluation — exactly the kind most teams never have time to do. Benchmarks six actively maintained, self-hostable open-source flow collectors (Akvorado, GoFlow2, IPFIXcol2, nfdump, pmacct, SiLK) for sFlow and IPFIX.
- **What**: Combines a feature/usability matrix (protocol support, enrichment, anonymization, Kafka/file outputs, telemetry endpoints) with sustainable-throughput benchmarks in a controlled testbed (synthetic generator, 20 flows per packet, 4 CPU cores / 12 GB RAM). Headline numbers: to Kafka, pmacct sustains ~186–209k flows/s, IPFIXcol2 ~375–446k, GoFlow2 ~13–15k (JSON) vs. ~77–86k (protobuf), Akvorado ~510–548k (raw forwarding); to file, nfdump reaches up to ~842k flows/s (IPFIX) and IPFIXcol2 ~680k. Main findings: input format matters (IPFIXcol2 and nfdump do best on IPFIX; SiLK and pmacct lag on sFlow), output serialization is crucial (GoFlow2's JSON path is an order of magnitude slower than protobuf), and message brokers enable horizontal scaling and redundant deployments.
- **How**: Found the *sustainable* processing rate by ramping packet rate while verifying flows in equals flows out; also documents production drop-detection tactics (canary flows, indirect CPU/memory/interface monitoring) since most collectors lack adequate telemetry endpoints.
- **Where applicable**: Results are dimensioned to a specific testbed (4 cores, 20 flows/packet — decapsulation overhead rises with fewer flows per packet) and the authors flag open questions (custom IPFIX fields, resource scaling). The load-testing tool is open-sourced (go-nflow-stresstest on Codeberg), so the benchmark is reproducible. No single winner — the value is the selection/dimensioning guidance.

### Procurement of dark fiber and optical spectrum for the new research network in Norway. Strategies and learnings

**Links:** [Abstract](https://tnc26.geant.org/programme/#Thursday/all/s1080) · [Slides](https://indico.geant.org/event/6/contributions/606/attachments/515/1140/2026-06-11%20Sikt%20-%20Nextgen%20Network%20procurement%20-%20TNC26%20-%20Olaf%20Schjelderup%20v3.pptx)

Olaf Schjeldrup (Sikt)

- **Why**: Physical WAN infrastructure strategy with real numbers and two years of negotiation scar tissue — squarely relevant to anyone whose job includes DC/WAN interconnect economics. Norway's NREN renewal covers ~13,000 km of renewed optical long-haul topology across punishing geography (3,000 km north–south, 103,000 km of coastline), with geopolitical tension explicitly cited as a driver for higher validated redundancy.
- **What**: Requirements: national coverage, terabit capacities with a preference order of dark fiber > optical spectrum (1–1.2 THz, flexigrid, license-free) > waves; 20-year IRU matching Open Line System lifetime; fiber sensing and bidirectional time-and-frequency transfer capability; extra fiber pairs reserved for research (quantum, sensing). Process: RFI spring 2023, RFQ end-2023, award criteria 60% quality / 40% TCO, 10 tenderers (3 hybrid fiber+spectrum, 7 fiber-only), negotiated procedure running over two years with six offer/negotiation phases, BAFO May 2025, award January 2026 to 8 sub-providers, main contracts May 2026, rollout 2026–28 — with "huge cost savings, increased optical footprint and redundancy."
- **How**: The evaluation complexity (choosing one path alters preferences for many others) was made manageable by dividing the country into 5 regions, evaluating inter-regional legs with add/drops at essential locations, backed by purpose-built evaluation models. Tactical tips: dark-fiber monopolies are consolidating and prices are rising on datacenter-industry demand; asking for both fiber and spectrum widens real competition; spectrum providers with greenfield DWDM plans will install L-band sensing and bidirectional T&F channels for you; multi-subprovider strategies cost more effort but save money and add diversity.
- **Where applicable**: Norwegian market and geography, but the procurement playbook (competition structure, evaluation decomposition, IRU terms, research fiber pairs) transfers to any large fiber buyer. Explicitly org-specific in outcomes; the strategy is the export.

### Time Travels in the L-Band: Deploying a National White Rabbit Infrastructure over Active DWDM Networks

**Links:** [Abstract](https://tnc26.geant.org/programme/#Thursday/all/s1080) · [Slides](https://indico.geant.org/event/6/contributions/607/attachments/415/1006/CESNET_TNC26-Time-Travels-in-L-Band-Final.pdf)

Michal Hazlinsky (CESNET)

- **Why**: A blueprint for running nanosecond-grade time distribution *on* an active, revenue-carrying DWDM network instead of dedicated dark fiber — plus one of the best examples of real NetDevOps automation at the optical layer (L0).
- **What**: CESNET distributes time traceable to UTC(TP) with better than 1 ns stability to 22 locations, with White Rabbit running in the L-band (1570/1571/1572 nm) coexisting with 8 C-band data channels on the same fiber of its 5,700 km multivendor network (Cisco, Ribbon/ECI, CzechLight). Spectrum-sharing was designed in from procurement: custom channel filters per line (8 C + 4 L channels, <1 dB insertion loss), bidirectional single-fiber transmission to defeat WR's path-asymmetry problem, and CzechLight SDN bidirectional EDFAs for long-haul.
- **How**: Open-source SDN-controlled BiDi amplifiers (NETCONF/RESTCONF, YANG model, IETF YANG-Push streaming telemetry with sub-second latencies) managed via Ansible with a NetBox inventory — actual DevOps playbooks at the photonic layer. - **Where applicable**: Requires a spectrum-sharing design (reserved L-band channels and filters) — cheapest on greenfield builds — and CESNET's own CzechLight hardware, but the hardware designs and software are open. The calibration methods and the L-band coexistence technique are broadly reusable; "standard APIs are MUST HAVE" is the summary lesson.

### Traffic at 3am: Armed with a Toothpick against AI Scrapers

**Links:** [Abstract](https://tnc26.geant.org/programme/#Thursday/all/s1155) · [Slides](https://indico.geant.org/event/6/contributions/680/attachments/427/920/Cheryl%20Andrea%20Fernando_Traffic%20at%203am.pptx)

Cheryl Andrea Fernando (SURF)

- **Why**: AI training scrapers are now an operational cost and risk problem for anyone serving public content — and the defensive landscape is fragmenting fast. Lightning-talk depth, but a useful map.
- **What**: A survey of mitigation strategies: robots.txt (voluntary, widely ignored), Cloudflare's AI Labyrinth (trapping non-compliant crawlers), monetization via Cloudflare Pay-per-Crawl and Akamai's Tolbit/Skyfire, open-source countermeasures like Anubis (proof-of-work gating that makes bot traffic expensive) and tarpits (Nepenthes, Quixotic, Iocane — feeding Markov babble to poison training data, with real caveats: SEO damage and self-inflicted resource costs). - **Where applicable**: General web-serving context, NREN-specific only in the tension between openness (discoverability) and protection (research data).

### From Public Cloud AI to National Infrastructure: Lessons from Sunet Scribe

**Links:** [Abstract](https://tnc26.geant.org/programme/#Thursday/all/s1083) · [Slides](https://indico.geant.org/event/6/contributions/614/attachments/444/1152/sunet_scribe_tnc26.pdf)

Erik Lundberg (Sunet)

- **Why**: A rare, fully-costed-in-public view of running a national AI inference service on a small GPU fleet — the operational reality behind "sovereign AI services."
- **What**: Sunet Scribe, a self-hosted, Whisper-based speech-to-text service inside the Swedish NREN, born because institutions didn't trust commercial providers with sensitive recordings and commercial STT wasn't optimized for Nordic languages (the fine-tuned Swedish model comes from the Royal Library). ### Toward Scalable Quantum Networks: Lessons from InterQnet

**Links:** [Abstract](https://tnc26.geant.org/programme/#Wednesday/all/s1072) · [Slides](https://indico.geant.org/event/6/contributions/590/attachments/504/1145/TNC26_InterQnet_Kettimuthu_V4.pptx)

- **Why**: Not the PM's daily bread, but a two-year progress report on the US DOE's full-stack quantum networking program that frames quantum networks as an *operations* problem — and argues NRENs already own the prerequisites (deployed fiber, automation, synchronization, optical-channel engineering).
- **What**: InterQnet runs two tightly coupled goals. InterQnet-Achieve: a three-node heterogeneous quantum network (Yb atom arrays, Er³⁺ ions, superconducting qubits) over deployed campus fiber with dark-fiber paths toward Fermilab, UChicago, and StarLight, using frequency conversion and microwave-to-optical transduction, under centralized SDN-style orchestration with automated calibration. ### Instant Data Structures via Shared Memory

**Links:** [Abstract](https://tnc26.geant.org/programme/#Wednesday/all/s1076) · [Slides](https://indico.geant.org/event/6/contributions/685/attachments/452/1064/TNC26_Instant_Data_Structures.pptx)

Roman Janota (CESNET)

- **Why**: A small systems idea with an outsized, verified result — and it generalizes well beyond network tooling.
- **What**: The boot-up bottleneck of network management systems: every tool re-parses hundreds of device configuration files to build its structural map of the network, serially, on every start. CESNET's answer: parse once, share the result. The first program to start builds the data structure, publishes a handle ("link") to it in a shared-memory region; every subsequent program checks for an existing link and skips parsing entirely. Changes (e.g. a new node) are published by generating a *new* link to the changed structure and atomically replacing the old one, with consumers checking for a newer link on access — a copy-on-write publication pattern.
- **How**: Plain shared memory plus handle indirection; no exotic infrastructure. Benchmarks show boot-up times up to 400×+ faster than re-parsing, with the gap so large the improved bars are barely visible on the charts.
- **Where applicable**: General technique for any tool family that re-derives the same large, mostly-static data structure from source files at startup — network inventory, config linting, query engines. Linux-centric; the pattern is cheap to adopt.

## Themes

- **AI is moving from demo to production ops, with safety architecture as the differentiator**: ESnet's ORBIT (prompts-as-code, human-in-the-loop), GÉANT's Saz (verifier-agent-over-planner-agent), Internet2/SURF's A2A agent federation, and RNP's no-data-scientist anomaly detection all share a stance: modest, auditable, incrementally-adopted AI that augments operators rather than replacing them.
- **The optical layer is becoming a multi-service, programmable substrate**: coherent DSPs doing topology tomography, White Rabbit coexisting with 400G in the L-band, fiber sensing and T&F channels written into procurement requirements (Sikt), and a 498 km ±100 ps field trial — the physical plant is being asked to do sensing, timing, and data at once.
- **Kubernetes (and licensing shock) is reshaping NREN service platforms**: RENATER's VMware→Talos/KubeVirt migration, nmaas multi-cluster edge orchestration, and RNP's Internal Developer Platform — with Broadcom's ESXi licensing changes explicitly cited as the trust-breaking trigger.
- **AI workloads are rewriting WAN requirements**: ESnet7 planning 50–70% above historical growth trend, negotiable service guarantees for agentic/interactive science, traffic classification and congestion prediction as first-class network functions — the "AI WAN" problem arriving from the science side, not just hyperscaler DCs.
- **Federated, open, community-owned tooling as the response to scale**: flow-collector benchmarking, HAWAT's federated Looking Glass, the Internet2/CAIDA BGP toolset and prefix registry, and open-source throughout (Churn, nmaas, CzechLight, Sunet Scribe) — collective infrastructure no single operator could justify building alone.
- **Sovereignty as an engineering constraint**: terrestrial time as GNSS backup, national self-hosted AI services, dark-fiber/spectrum procurement strategy, and EU-backed submarine cables — trust and geopolitical redundancy are now design inputs, not afterthoughts.
